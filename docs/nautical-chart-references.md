# Nautical-Chart Cartography — Reference Material

Primary sources on how nautical charts represent depth (soundings, contours, depth areas,
safety depth, datums) and how bathymetry is generalized for charting. Compiled for the
chart-data-model work (chart datum [#16](https://github.com/openwatersio/seascape/issues/16), soundings/safety-depth, confidence & provenance [#17](https://github.com/openwatersio/seascape/issues/17)); useful to any
agent touching `pipelines/soundings_run.py`, `pipelines/contour_run.py`, `pipelines/depare_run.py`,
`pipelines/smooth.py`, datum handling, or the viewer's chart styling.

**Verification status** — each source is marked:

- ✅ **read** — the primary document was fetched and read; direct quotes below are verbatim from it.
- ◑ **citation-verified** — bibliographic details confirmed (Crossref / publisher / open abstract), but the full text was **not** accessed here (paywall or bot-block, noted); claims are from the abstract or corroborated by a ✅ source.

## Actionable takeaways (each backed by a source below)

- **Round to the shoaler side, always.** Soundings round down, drying heights round up (IHO S-4 B-412 ✅). We floor depth toward shallower — keep it.
- **Shoal-biased selection.** Generalization must never let a mariner interpolate a depth _deeper_ than the source shows — IHO S-4's "triangular method" (Triangle + Edge tests) ✅; Zoraster & Bayer select prime and background soundings "with a shoal bias" ✅.
- **Density widens with depth; background pattern is a regular lattice.** Zoraster & Bayer, verbatim: background soundings "present a **regular pattern** … **triangular or rectangular** with the **pacing between soundings increasing with depth**" ✅ (a quincunx/rhomboidal lattice is exactly this). IHO S-4 B-410d: flat areas get few, evenly-spaced soundings "gradually becoming more widely spaced as the depth increases" ✅. NOAA's Nautical Chart Manual quantifies legible spacing at chart scale — **critical ≥ 6 mm apart, supporting ≥ 10 mm, fill 15–30 mm** (quoted verbatim in Skopeliti et al. 2020 ✅).
- **Zoom-out drops the deeper soundings first.** ENC does this with **SCAMIN** — a per-feature minimum-display-scale attribute (S-57 §2.2.7 ✅) ≈ our per-feature tippecanoe `minzoom`. Assign it shoal-biased.
- **True position is the chart rule.** Soundings sit at their surveyed location; paper charts allow out-of-position _least-depth_ soundings only, flagged with a pointer/brackets (S-4 B-412 ✅); **ENC forbids out-of-position entirely** — "There is no 'sounding, out of position' in an ENC" (S-57 UOC B-412.2 ✅). A jittered/quincunx grid is a raster-derived "background sounding" approximation, not chart-authentic prime selection.
- **Radius method, ~free via tippecanoe.** Pre-sort soundings shoalest-first, then `--drop-densest-as-needed` (spacing grows as needed) + `-pi` (preserve input order) keeps the shoalest and thins the dense deeper fill — the classic radius method, densifying with zoom (tippecanoe docs ✅; mechanism matches Zoraster "eliminate nearby soundings to avoid overcrowding" ✅ and Skopeliti's "buffer radius" ✅).
- **Keep-shoalest on label collision.** MapLibre `symbol-sort-key = depth` (ascending) + `text-allow-overlap: false` → shallowest placed first, wins the collision (style spec ✅). We already do this.
- **Safety depth (feeds the viewer's safety-depth shading).** S-52 emphasizes soundings ≤ the mariner-set safety depth and highlights the safety contour (◑).
- **Smoothing must be shoal-biased, and the standard says so outright.** S-4 B-411.5, verbatim: "Where necessary, smoothing will include deeper water within shoaler contours" ✅. Any symmetric kernel in the shallow band violates this.
- **Generalize the surface *or* generalize the contours — both are accepted, and the choice has a stated trade-off.** Skopeliti et al. 2021 §2.2 ✅: surface-first "is robust, fast and allows depth contours to be generated at various scales"; contour-first is "more reliable, thus contributing to the safety of navigation." Two hydrographic offices document surface-first in production (AHO ✅, IHM ✅). This pipeline is surface-first.
- **Shoalest-per-cell gridding does NOT guarantee safe contours.** Peters et al. §2 ✅, on max rasterization specifically: contour extraction interpolates between cell centres, so "this easily results in safety violations at 'secondary' local maxima in a single grid cell," worsening with cell size. Per-pixel shoal bias ≠ per-contour shoal bias. Assert against the source raster, or classify-then-polygonize for a pixel-exact guarantee.
- **Never delete an isolated shoal or islet — exaggerate it.** Four independent sources ✅◑: isolated features below the minimum size tolerance are enlarged to it, never dropped. A morphological max/closing does this natively; a vector area sieve does the opposite.
- **Pits and peaks are not symmetric.** Guilbert & Zhang 2012 ✅: "A pit cannot be enlarged or aggregated; A too small or not relevant pit is removed; A peak cannot be removed; A too small peak is enlarged or aggregated with an adjacent peak."
- **Argue thresholds in millimetres at map scale, not pixel factors.** The published minimums are all mm-at-scale and transfer to any zoom — see the table in §E.
- **Group 1 objects (DEPARE, LNDARE, DRGARE) may not carry SCAMIN** (S-57 UOC ✅). An ENC cannot declutter depth areas by hiding them; scale is handled by compiling a separate generalized cell per usage band. That is the standards-level reason per-zoom *geometry* generalization is the only available lever.
- **The marsh coastline is officially approximate.** S-57 UOC 4.7.3 ✅ requires `COALNE`/`CATCOA = 8` with `QUAPOS = 4` (approximate) where a marsh meets the coastline. Resolving marsh edges to survey resolution asserts a precision the standard explicitly disclaims.

## A. Primary standards (IHO / NOAA / NGA)

- ✅ **IHO S-4 — Regulations for INT Charts and Chart Specifications**, Ed. 4.9.0, Mar 2021. Part B §400, regs **B-410…B-413**: shoal-biased triangular selection, density by seabed type, least-depth rules, true-position placement, shoaler-side rounding. https://iho.int/uploads/user/pubs/standards/s-4/S4_V4-9-0_March_2021.pdf
- ✅ **IHO S-4 — the generalization regulations**, read from Ed. 4.8.0. The area/contour counterparts to B-410…B-413, none of which were previously catalogued here:
  - **B-411.5** — the shoal-biased smoothing mandate (quoted in the takeaways), plus "the intricacy of contours gives some guidance on the adequacy of the survey."
  - **B-403.1(d)** — group typification: "where they occur in groups a selection of representative symbols is permissible, showing the outermost ones as individually as space permits."
  - **B-312.2** — marsh depiction: "either by marsh symbols or, exceptionally, by a legend"; and where the HW line is indeterminate, "an approximate coastline should be charted at the outer limits of vegetation emerging at high water" (the apparent shoreline).
  - **B-421.1** — the one explicit IHO minimum dimension: scattered islets in a foul area shown as black dots "ideally with no dimension less than 0,5mm."
  - **B-417.6 / B-418.1** — "areas delimited by a bold line," licensed as "most appropriate in inshore waters such as coastal archipelagos and barrier reefs": the paper-chart licence to stop resolving a mosaic and draw its envelope.
  - _Practical note:_ use the **4.8.0** PDF for automated extraction. The 4.9.0 PDF's bold runs are ASCII-shifted by +33 and extract as garbage. https://iho.int/iho_pubs/standard/S-4/S4_V4-8-0_Oct_2018_EN.pdf
- ✅ **IHO S-57 Appendix B.1 Annex A — Use of the Object Catalogue (UOC)**, Ed. 4.3.0, Oct 2022. `SOUNDG` (multipoint), **`SCAMIN`** (§2.2.7, minimum display scale / decluttering), `EXPSOU` (shoaler-than-surrounding), "there is no 'sounding, out of position' in an ENC" (B-412.2). https://iho.int/uploads/user/pubs/standards/s-57/S-57%20Appendix%20B.1%20Annex%20A_UOC_Ed%204.3.0_Final.pdf · base standard Ed. 3.1.0 https://iho.int/uploads/user/pubs/standards/s-57/31Main.pdf
- ✅ **IHO S-57 UOC — the area-generalization clauses.** **§4.7.3**: a marsh behind the coastline is `LNDRGN`/`CATLND = 2`; where its seaward edge is the coastline, `COALNE`/`CATCOA = 8` (marshy shore) with the spatial object attributed **`QUAPOS = 4` (approximate)**. **§5.8.3.1 "areas of omitted bathymetry"**: examine the larger-scale cells for the shallowest non-intertidal `DEPARE`, generalize the intertidal from the larger-scale coverage, and "one DEPARE object may then be created" carrying those `DRVAL1`/`DRVAL2` — under a `CTNARE` caution area. Also: intertidal is `DEPARE` with `DRVAL1 = −H, DRVAL2 = 0` (`ITDARE` was deleted from the catalogue), and Group 1 objects may not carry `SCAMIN`.
- ✅ **IHO S-58 — ENC Validation Checks**, Ed. 7.0.0. **Check 571** (Warning): "For each edge which contains vertices at a density Greater than 0.3mm at compilation scale" → "Generalise edge(s)." The only hard numeric geometry rule in the ENC standards, and the one this pipeline is closest to violating at z14 and below. NOAA works at 0.4 mm in projected space to guarantee 0.3 mm on output (Nyberg et al., IHR 2020).
- ✅ **US Chart No. 1 — the marsh/indeterminate-shoreline symbols.** **C 33** Marsh/Swamp/Reed beds, with a dashed variant for "Marsh with coastline or shoreline construction of low accuracy in position"; supplementary national symbols **(a)** "Chart sounding datum line (surveyed)" vs **(b)** "Approximate sounding datum line (inadequately surveyed)"; **(p)** "Apparent shoreline." The existing symbology for exactly the ambiguity a generalized marsh introduces.
- ◑ **IHO S-52 — Chart Content and Display Aspects of ECDIS** (+ Presentation Library), Ed. 6.1.1, Jun 2015. PDF confirmed live; safety-depth emphasis of soundings + safety-contour highlighting from the spec summary (not deep-read here). https://iho.int/uploads/user/pubs/standards/s-52/S-52%20Edition%206.1.1%20-%20June%202015.pdf
- ✅ **US Chart No. 1** (NOAA + NGA) — Section I "Depths": sounding symbols, out-of-position sounding, drying heights, doubtful-quality markers (SD/ED/Rep/PA/PD). https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/docs/us-chart-1/ChartNo1.pdf
- ✅ **NOAA Nautical Chart User's Manual**, 1st ed. 1997. Shoal-bias selection, chart-user facing: soundings notably shoaler/deeper than their surroundings are treated as "important" and usually selected; a shoal's least depth is charted first, then "supportive soundings and curves are then selected to reinforce this least depth." https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/49555
- ◑ **NOAA Nautical Chart Manual, Vol. 1 — Policies and Procedures** — the internal production manual (distinct from the User's Manual above). Its legible sounding-spacing figures (critical ≥ 6 mm, supporting ≥ 10 mm, fill 15–30 mm at chart scale) are quoted verbatim in Skopeliti et al. 2020 ✅; the manual's own PDF bot-blocks automated fetch. Landing: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/49956

## B. Sounding-selection algorithms (cartographic literature)

- ✅ **Zoraster, S. & Bayer, S. (1992), "Automated cartographic sounding selection,"** International Hydrographic Review LXIX(1), March 1992 (open-access reprint, pp. 103ff). Origin of the **radius method**, developed for the US National Ocean Service (NOAA/NOS). Classifies soundings **prime / background / limiting**; prime soundings "deviate significantly in depth from the bottom depth which would be expected based on linear interpolation between chart depth curves" and are "selected with a shoal bias"; background soundings form a regular triangular/rectangular lattice with spacing increasing with depth; as soundings are chosen, "nearby soundings … are eliminated from further consideration to avoid overcrowding." Read from the scan at https://journals.lib.unb.ca/index.php/ihr/article/download/23255/27030/0
- ✅ **Du, J., Lu, Y. & Zhai, J. (2001), "A Model of Sounding Generalization Based on Recognition of Terrain Features,"** Proc. 20th ICC, Beijing. TIN + "sounding tree"; adopts prime/background/limiting; depth-scaled background spacing (1–1.5 cm <20 m … up to 3 cm >50 m); always keep deepest+shallowest per subarea. https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2001/icc2001/file/f04006.pdf
- ✅ **Peters, R.Y., Ledoux, H. & Meijers, B.M. (2013), "Generation and generalization of safe depth-contours for hydrographic charts using a surface-based approach,"** Proc. 16th ICA Generalisation Workshop, Dresden (TU Delft). The four constraints (attributed to Zhang & Guilbert 2011) — **safety, legibility, topology, morphology**; safety, verbatim: "At every location, the indicated depth may never be deeper than the depth that was originally measured at that location"; contours may be moved only toward greater depth. Voronoi natural-neighbour surface → extract safe contours. **§2 also refutes shoalest-per-cell gridding as a safety mechanism** — see the takeaways; and §3.2's upward-only-lifting Laplace interpolant is the constructively-safe alternative: "all modifications in the shape of the surface are upwards, which implies that the safety constraint is respected at all times," with aggregation and omission emerging from 100× iteration while local maxima are preserved (Fig. 6). https://gdmc.nl/publications/2013/Safe_depth-contours_hydrographic_charts.pdf
- ✅ **Guilbert, E. & Zhang, X. (2012), "Generalisation of submarine features on nautical charts,"** ISPRS Annals I-2:13–18. Open access, and the readable one-page statement of the constraint set that Guilbert 2016 sits behind a paywall to repeat. The five functional constraints verbatim: an isobath may only move toward greater depths; may only be removed if the new representation is not deeper; "adjacent isobaths can be merged if they are at the same depth and the area in between is deeper"; "**isobaths delineating fairways must be preserved**"; isobaths characterising features not relevant for navigation can be removed. Plus the pit/peak asymmetry (in the takeaways) and the legibility floor: "a feature on the chart must be large enough to contain a sounding marking its deepest or shallowest point." https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/I-2/13/2012/isprsannals-I-2-13-2012.pdf
- ◑ **Dyer, N., Kastrisios, C. & De Floriani, L. (2022), "Label-based generalization of bathymetry data for hydrographic sounding selection,"** Cartography and GIS 49(4). **Label-based** selection — thin by the printed label's footprint so labels don't over-plot; shoal-biased; best on safety+legibility vs radius/grid. Abstract read; full text paywalled. DOI 10.1080/15230406.2021.2014974 · https://scholars.unh.edu/ccom/1505/ · open-source tool https://github.com/NoelDyer/Sounding-Selection
- ◑ **Kastrisios, C., Calder, B. et al. (2019), "Towards automated validation of charted soundings,"** Geo-spatial Information Science. Triangle test + Edge test for shoal-bias validation; proposes the Nautical Surface Test. Abstract read; full text paywalled. DOI 10.1080/10095020.2019.1618636 · https://scholars.unh.edu/ccom/1736/
- ◑ **Guilbert, E. (2016), "Feature-Driven Generalization of Isobaths on Nautical Charts: A Multi-Agent System Approach,"** Transactions in GIS 20(2) (Crossref-verified title/venue/year). Generalizes **depth contours** (not point soundings); safety imposes a deformation direction — a contour may move only toward the **shoaler** side. Relevant to contour smoothing (M4.3). Paywalled. DOI 10.1111/tgis.12147
- ✅ **Skopeliti, A., Stamou, L. & Tsoulos, L. (2020), "Generalization of Soundings across Scales: From DTM to Harbour and Approach Nautical Charts,"** ISPRS IJGI 9(11):693. Radius method, verbatim: soundings "located inside the circle and the others will be eliminated (i.e., buffer radius)"; quotes NOAA's Nautical Chart Manual for the mm spacing figures (critical ≥ 6 mm / supporting ≥ 10 mm / fill 15–30 mm apart, supportive 40–100% of fill spacing). Read from the open-access PDF: https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/9/11/693 (PDF https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijgi/ijgi-09-00693/article_deploy/ijgi-09-00693-v2.pdf)
- ✅ **Kastrisios, C., Dyer, N., Nada, T., Calder, B. et al. (2025), "Sailing the Rough Seas of Automated ENC Compilation,"** ICA Advances 5:17. Sounding taxonomy (prime/least-depth/shoal/deep/supporting/fill), safety constraint, surface test, two-stage (hydrographic then cartographic) selection. https://ica-adv.copernicus.org/articles/5/17/2025/ica-adv-5-17-2025.pdf
- ✅ **Dyer, N., Kastrisios, C. & De Floriani, L. (2023), "Chart features, data quality, and scale in cartographic sounding selection from composite bathymetric data,"** Geo-spatial Information Science. Incorporates chart features (depth contours) and hydrographic-selection components into the cartographic selection process, and accounts for features "not considered for the sounding selection, such as wrecks, rocks, obstructions." Read from the author's open copy. DOI 10.1080/10095020.2023.2266222 · https://geovis.umiacs.io/publication/dyer-2023-chart/dyer-2023-chart.pdf
- ✅ **CCOM/UNH Joint Hydrographic Center — Hydrographic Sounding Selection project.** Definition of hydrographic selection ("shoal-biased and dense, yet manageable, subset"); radius/grid methods criticized as parameter-sensitive; label-based method + density-reduction tables (e.g. Charleston Harbor 221,494 → 1,345 soundings). https://www.ccom.unh.edu/research/research-areas/nautical-cartography/project-hydrographic-sounding-selection

## B2. Area & surface generalization (marsh, tidal flats, islet mosaics)

Compiled 2026-07-30 for the shallow-band work ([`plans/2026-07-30-shallow-coarsening.md`](plans/2026-07-30-shallow-coarsening.md)). Section B above covers point soundings; this covers the depth-**area** and surface side, which the doc previously had nothing on.

**Production workflows at hydrographic offices**

- ✅ **Rustomji, P. (2019), "Semi-automated generation of depth contours for ENCs,"** International Hydrographic Review. The Australian Hydrographic Office's CARIS BASE Editor workflow, with parameters — the closest published match to surface-first generalization in a real chart pipeline. Five steps: re-grid at "three pixels per mm at compilation scale" using CARIS's **"shoalest depth true position"** ("re-gridding is done by selecting the shoalest depth within each output grid cell" — block max, by name); shoal expansion to a "minimum target diameter of approximately 4 mm at compilation scale" via a 3 mm radius; Laplacian smoothing that "raises the elevation of grid cells" and "infills low areas", 1–20 iterations; contour the generalized surface; then **delete tiny _deep_ closed contours only**, below a 10 mm-diameter-at-scale area (≈160,000 m² at 1:45,000). https://ihr.iho.int/articles/semi-automated-generation-of-depth-contours-for-encs/
- ✅ **Manzano, J. (2021), "The bathymetric compilation, a true challenge in the nautical chart generation process,"** International Hydrographic Review (Instituto Hidrográfico de la Marina, Spain). Independent corroboration: "creating a 'generalised surface' from the final combined surface at the same scale of the compilation," contours "smoothed in different passes … as well as merging those tiny, closed contours," with "a shoal-biased pattern (including deeper water within shoaler contour) to ensure the safety constraint." https://ihr.iho.int/articles/the-bathymetric-compilation-a-true-challenge-in-the-nautical-chart-generation-process/
- ✅ **Skopeliti, A., Tsoulos, L. & Pe'eri, S. (2021), "Depth Contours and Coastline Generalization for Harbour and Approach Nautical Charts,"** ISPRS IJGI 10(4):197. The surface-first vs contour-first framing (quoted in the takeaways); the **double-buffering** operator, "commonly employed in commercial hydrographic software … It effectively takes into account the safety constraint as well as performs a form of aggregation"; and NOAA's `Rescheme Cook Book` islet rules quoted verbatim — **0.8 mm minimum islet dimension** ("8 m for 1:10 K, 16 m for 1:20 K, 32 m for 1:40 K and 64 m for 1:80 K"), below which an islet collapses to a point or line, and within 0.8 mm of shore it merges to the shoreline with the modified shoreline on the outer boundary. https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijgi/ijgi-10-00197/article_deploy/ijgi-10-00197.pdf
- ◑ **Kastrisios, C. et al. (2025), "Sailing the Rough Seas of Automated ENC Compilation,"** ICA Adv. 5:17 — already cited in §B for soundings; its **island operator cascade** is the current state of practice: "isolated islands are retained in the output as they present navigational significance. Isolated islands smaller than a minimum size tolerance are exaggerated to the minimum size for the target scale. Small near-islands with dimensions below a predefined tolerance are collapsed to points. Those that exceed the dimension tolerance and are closer to larger islands than a near distance threshold, are amalgamated."
- ◑ **Nada, T., Kastrisios, C. et al. (2025), "Automated islands generalization techniques for nautical charts,"** Geo-spatial Information Science. Voronoi + buffer hybrid for islet mosaics; abstract only, paywalled. DOI 10.1080/10095020.2025.2568115

**The operator has a name, and it is amalgamation, not aggregation**

- ✅ **Regnauld, N. (2003), "Algorithms for the Amalgamation of Topographic Data,"** Proc. 21st ICC, Durban. §3.3 **amalgamation by flooding** is the exact operator for a mosaic of sub-threshold patches: "replacing a group of features by a single polygon. The boundary of the resulting polygon [is] related to the extent of the initial group rather than the geometry of the initial features. This is useful to cope with dense areas of small objects." Also the caveat to heed before reaching for morphology: "Morphologic operators are very hard to fully control. They generate global solution with no easy local tuning." https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2003/Papers/026.pdf
- ◑ **Müller, J.-C. & Wang, Z. (1992), "Area-patch generalisation: a competitive approach,"** Cartographic Journal 29(2):137–144. Origin of the term *area-patch generalization* — the founding paper for this exact phenomenon.
- ◑ **Shea, K.S. & McMaster, R.B. (1989), "Cartographic generalization in a digital environment,"** Proc. Auto-Carto 9. p. 62 gives the textbook case: "an area containing numerous small lakes — each too small to be depicted separately — could with a judicious combination of the areas, retain the original map characteristic." Note the taxonomy: this is **amalgamation/merge** (many→one, same dimension); *aggregation* is the point→area dimension change, which is a different operator.
- ◑ **Su, B., Li, Z., Lodwick, G. & Müller, J.-C. (1997), "Algebraic models for the aggregation of area features based upon morphological operators,"** IJGIS 11(3). Formalizes closing/opening (buffer-then-shrink) for area aggregation: "the process of area aggregation is decomposed into two components, viz., combination and shape refinement." Descends from Perkal's ε-generalization (1966).
- ✅ **Anderson-Tarver, C., Leyk, S. & Buttenfield, B. (2010), "Automated Generalization of NHD Swamp/Marsh Features,"** AutoCarto. The only paper on generalizing swamp/marsh polygons specifically (NHD 1:24,000 → 1:100,000, Florida–Georgia). Names the failure mode to guard against: "features whose size is below the defined constraint but collectively could constitute significant features are not included." Fuzzy membership over area (24,000–64,500 m²), inter-polygon distance (50–250 m; membership = 1 below 50 m, where "features are assumed to merge"), and neighbour count within 600 m. https://isprs.org/proceedings/XXXVIII/part4/files/Anderson-Tarver.pdf

**Minimum-dimension thresholds, with primary sources**

- ✅ **USGS, *Standards for the National Hydrography Dataset* (1999)**, p. 2-107 — the only agency spec with an explicit marsh-hole rule, and the most directly applicable number found: capture SWAMP/MARSH if "≥ 0.24″ along the shortest axis"; "**break SWAMP/MARSH for clearings that are ≥ 0.05″ along the shortest axis, or for linear clearings that are ≥ 0.025″**" (i.e. fill every hole narrower than 1.27 mm at scale). Also **AREA OF COMPLEX CHANNELS** — a braided delta with "at least five subchannels" is captured as one area feature instead of resolving the channels. Plus the 0.03″ minimum island short axis. https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/atoms/files/NHD%201999%20Draft%20Standards%20-%20Capture%20conditions.PDF
- ✅ **NOAA CUSP shoreline mileage methodology** — "Streams that are connected to the shoreline, and are at least 0.5 mm wide, are considered to be shoreline themselves"; "At a map scale of 1:24,000, this is equivalent to 12 meters on the ground. Based on this rule, streams, rivers and other waterways that were less than 12 meters wide were removed." https://shoreline.noaa.gov/mileage.html
- ◑ **Galanda, M. (2003), *Automated Polygon Generalization in a Multi Agent System*** (PhD, Univ. Zürich), Table 6.1 p. 69 — the citable metric constraint set for area patches, all in map mm: vertex spacing > 0.1, min shape width > 0.6, min shape height > 0.4, narrowness > 0.6, **min area > 4 mm²** (constraint M4, for a solid-fill polygonal subdivision — the right family for a tinted depth band), object separation > 0.6. https://www.geo.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:77c11613-a0f5-4b10-8dd9-491cf17527d3/thesis_MartinGalanda_2003.pdf
- **⚠ 0.4 mm² is folklore — do not cite it.** It has no primary source. Spiess (1990) states 0.4 mm of minimum *linear* extent for a black-on-white area and someone squared it. The real published minimum *areas* are Töpfer 1974 (0.25 mm²), Hake/Grünreich/Meng 2002 (1 mm² to read colour), and Galanda M4 (4 mm²).
- ◑ **Töpfer's radical law** (Töpfer & Pillewizer 1966) — `n_f = n_a · √(M_a/M_f)^x`, x = 1/2/3 for point/line/**areal** symbols. Volume sanity check: one zoom step out (2× scale) keeps 35% of areal features, two steps (4×) keeps 12.5%. If per-zoom output is not dropping roughly an order of magnitude every two zooms, the generalization is too timid.

**Depiction of ambiguity as a deliberate choice**

- ◑ **Raposo, P., Brewer, C. & Sparks, K. (2016)** — deliberately ragged/fuzzy rendering for features whose boundary is genuinely indeterminate, marsh included. Peer-reviewed precedent for an honest imprecise edge rather than a crisp false one; relevant if the shallow band moves to classify-then-polygonize and inherits a pixel staircase.
- **Comparative practice.** Natural Earth publishes an explicit generalization philosophy; OSM Carto, protomaps-basemaps and the MapLibre/Mapbox standard styles render `natural=wetland` as a fill+pattern without aggregating geometry, so none of them is a source for thresholds. The Murray et al. Global Intertidal Change dataset (30 m Landsat-derived) is the closest published generalized intertidal product and its resolution choice is a data-availability artifact, not a cartographic one.

## D. Point distribution & per-zoom thinning (technique)

- ✅ **Red Blob Games — jittered grid / Poisson disk / blue noise** (Amit Patel). A single jitter scalar can't be both isotropic and clump-free — jittered grid reads gridded _or_ clumps; Poisson-disk/blue-noise enforce a minimum spacing. https://www.redblobgames.com/x/1830-jittered-grid/
- ◑ **Mitchell's Best Candidate blue noise** (demofox) — deterministic, incremental, seedable. https://blog.demofox.org/2017/10/20/generating-blue-noise-sample-points-with-mitchells-best-candidate-algorithm/
- ◑ **Bridson fast Poisson-disk** (O(n) via active list + grid). https://github.com/martynafford/poisson-disc-distribution-bridson
- ◑ **R2 / golden-ratio quasirandom blue-noise sequence** (Martin Roberts) — deterministic _progressive_ sequence (add points in order to densify — a fit for densify-with-zoom). https://extremelearning.com.au/a-simple-method-to-construct-isotropic-quasirandom-blue-noise-point-sequences/
- ✅ **tippecanoe** — `--drop-densest-as-needed` (≈ radius method, spacing grows to fit), `-r`/`--drop-rate`, `-B`/`--base-zoom`, `-ah`/Hilbert order, **`-pi`/`--preserve-input-order`** (keep priority order → pre-sort shoalest-first). https://github.com/mapbox/tippecanoe
- ◑ **Felt — "How Tippecanoe makes your data look great at every scale."** Retained subset = uniform sample + points furthest from neighbours (blue-noise-ish); Hilbert order gives cheap spatial representativeness. https://felt.com/blog/tippecanoe-display-scale-precision
- ✅ **MapLibre Style Spec — symbols.** `symbol-sort-key` + `*-allow-overlap` collision behaviour; per-feature per-zoom `filter` (SCAMIN-style). https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/
